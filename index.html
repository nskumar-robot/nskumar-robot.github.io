Nithin Kumar

I am a PhD student in Mechanical Engineering at Vanderbilt University, where I work with Prof. Eric J. Barth. I develop models and controllers that make highly compliant, underactuated robots move accurately and dynamically in real time — combining first-principles mechanics with data-driven system identification, and validating everything on hardware I design and build.

My broader interest is in bridging physics-based and learned models for control of high-dimensional, compliant systems: retaining enough structure to reason about stability, observability, and energy consistency, while using data to capture what first principles cannot. My long-term goal is to close the gap between what compliant robots can physically do and what we can reliably command them to do. That means moving past quasi-static, free-space control and leveraging compliance for stable, dexterous interaction in unstructured environments.

I hold an S.M. in Mechanical Engineering from MIT, M.S. in Robotics Engineering from WPI, and a B.E. in Mechanical Engineering from Vanderbilt University.

Control-Oriented Learning for Dynamic Tracking and Stability Analysis of Soft Pneumatic Actuators
Nithin S. Kumar, Eric J. Barth
IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), [Year]
paper / arXiv / code / video

A control-oriented learning framework combines a nonlinear static model with learned residual dynamics for real-time 3D control of soft pneumatic actuators. Experiments achieve ~1 mm RMSE at 10 mm/s and <10 mm RMSE at 100 mm/s, while enabling stable user-guided motion exceeding 25 m/s² peak acceleration and real-time obstacle avoidance.

Task-space model-based control of pneumatic soft actuators
Nithin S. Kumar, Joshua Gaston, D. Caleb Rucker, Eric J. Barth
IEEE/ASME Transactions on Mechatronics, [Year]
paper / arXiv / code / video

A sparse, non-minimal discrete elastic rod model enables real-time dynamic task-space control of soft pneumatic actuators without data-driven model training. A 10-rod model and observer run at 1 kHz, achieving 1.5–2.3 mm RMSE on precision trajectories and stable tracking up to 2 Hz and 37 cm/s.

Constrained Lagrangian-Based, Nonholonomic Modeling and Validation of a Pneumatically Actuated Wheeled Soft Robot
Nithin S. Kumar, John E. Peters, Sung Y. Kim, Joshua Gaston, D. Caleb Rucker, Eric J. Barth
Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics, [Year]
paper / code / video

A constrained Lagrangian model incorporating holonomic and nonholonomic constraints captures the highly dynamic locomotion of a pneumatically actuated wheeled soft robot. The robot achieves speeds up to 22 cm/s (1.38 body lengths/s) and demonstrates forward, reverse, and circular locomotion, with the model reproducing configuration and velocity trends across three robot designs.

Data-driven dynamic modeling and control of pneumatic soft actuators with stability analysis
Nithin S. Kumar, John E. Peters, Thomas Beckers, Eric J. Barth
American Control Conference / ALDSC / [Venue], [Year]
paper / code / video

A control-oriented learning framework combines a sparse nonlinear feedforward map with EDMDc residual dynamics, enabling data-driven feedback control and model-based stability prediction without explicit first-principles dynamics. Experiments achieve 1.8–2.5 mm RMSE on shape-rich trajectories and <10 mm RMSE at tip speeds up to 104 mm/s.

Touching is Believing: Contact Sensing to Enhance Situational Awareness for Concentric Tube Robots
Nithin S. Kumar, Jason A. Shrand, Susheela Sharma, S. Duke Herrell III, Nicholas L. Kavoussi, Ahmed Ghazi, Eric J. Barth, Ipek Oguz, Alan Kuntz, Robert J. Webster III
Hamlyn Symposium on Medical Robotics (HSMR), 2025
paper / video

Impedance-based contact sensing enables a concentric tube robot to collect tissue surface points for robot-to-anatomy registration during endoscopic procedures. Experiments in an anthropomorphic prostate phantom achieve a mean surface registration error of 2.11 ± 1.01 mm, demonstrating touch sensing as an additional source of information for surgical situational awareness.
